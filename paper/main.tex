\begin{abstract}
Dense instrumentation of urban environments with sensors is limited by the cost, spatial heterogeneity, and failure of physical sensor nodes: the public reference air-quality network we study comprises deployments of just 40, 10, and 4 nodes across three Indian cities. We therefore ask not which model forecasts best, but \emph{how few sensor nodes a deployment needs} to sustain accurate spatiotemporal inference, and how far a small sensor budget can be stretched. We study three levers on a single inductive graph neural network whose ${\approx}24$k parameters are independent of the node count and which runs in under 3\,ms per inference on a commodity CPU. \emph{Virtual sensing:} by propagating information across the sensor graph, the model estimates the field at unsensored locations with \Rtwo up to 0.77, improving on a meteorology-only station-independent baseline by 0.08--0.16 \Rtwo --- software sensors that lower the physical-node budget. \emph{Cross-deployment cold-start transfer:} a newly deployed sparse network inherits a model pre-trained on a dense one and, with as little as 15\% of its own labels, recovers most attainable accuracy, verified against zero-shot and from-scratch controls in all 24 transfer settings. \emph{Graceful degradation:} inference accuracy at unsensored nodes stays above 0.80 \Rtwo even when the network is reduced to four sensors. We report two honest negatives --- a lightweight station-independent model matches the graph model on plain forecasting, and an advection--diffusion physics prior yields no significant gain --- and release CPU-only reproducible code. The study reframes spatiotemporal deep learning as a sensor-budget problem central to large-scale IoT sensing.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% =========================================================================
\IEEEPARstart{T}{he} value of an Internet-of-Things deployment rests on the spatial density of its sensors, yet density is exactly what is expensive to provision and maintain. Each physical node has acquisition, calibration, power, communication, and upkeep costs; coverage is therefore sparse, unevenly distributed, and degrades as nodes fail. The operative engineering question for a deployment is consequently not which model attains the lowest error given abundant data, but \emph{how few sensor nodes are enough}---and how much of the cost of additional nodes can be displaced into software that infers, transfers, and degrades gracefully.

We study this sensor-budget question on a demanding, real testbed: the public air-quality sensing networks of three Indian cities, exposed through the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) Continuous Ambient Air Quality Monitoring (CAAQM) system. The three deployments differ in size by an order of magnitude---Delhi 40 nodes, Kolkata 10, Guwahati 4 (Fig.~\ref{fig:study})---a natural stress test for sparse, heterogeneous IoT sensing. Fine particulate matter (\pmf) is the measured signal, a leading global health risk \cite{hei2024,who2021}; but it is the \emph{sensing constraint}, not the pollutant, that is the subject of this paper. The methods are signal-agnostic and apply to any spatially distributed sensing modality.

\textbf{The sensor-budget problem.} For a target inference accuracy, a deployment can be made cheaper along three axes (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}). (i) \emph{Virtual sensing}: estimate the signal at locations with \emph{no} physical sensor by exploiting spatial correlation, so a few physical nodes cover many points. (ii) \emph{Cold-start transfer}: bring a newly deployed sparse network online by inheriting a model trained on an existing dense deployment, rather than waiting to collect a long local history. (iii) \emph{Graceful degradation}: retain accuracy as sensors drop out. A model that delivers all three from a single, edge-deployable network directly reduces the number and cost of physical sensors a system must field.

\textbf{Why a graph, and an honest scope.} A station-independent recurrent model is a strong, lightweight forecaster, and we show it matches a graph model on plain per-node forecasting. But it is structurally blind to the three levers above, all of which require reasoning \emph{between} nodes. We therefore use an inductive spatio-temporal graph neural network (ST-GNN) whose parameter count is independent of the node count \V, so one ${\approx}24$k-parameter model runs verbatim on the 40-, 10-, and 4-node deployments at ${<}3$\,ms per inference on a commodity CPU. Crucially, the graph earns its keep \emph{precisely} at virtual sensing and sparsity robustness---not at forecasting, where the cheaper model suffices. We make this trade-off explicit rather than hiding it.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{C1 (Virtual sensing).} An inductive ST-GNN that infers the field at unsensored nodes from a sparse sensored set, improving on a meteorology-only station-independent baseline by 0.08--0.16 \Rtwo across deployments---a software substitute for physical sensors.
\item \textbf{C2 (Cold-start transfer).} A cross-deployment pre-train-and-fine-tune scheme in which a sparse network inherits a dense-network model and recovers most accuracy from ${\le}15\%$ of its own labels; benchmarked head-to-head against a station-independent LSTM transferer.
\item \textbf{C3 (Graceful degradation \& verification).} A sensor-budget characterization showing inference holds above 0.80 \Rtwo down to four nodes, under a strict three-way protocol that compares every transfer cell to both a zero-shot and a from-scratch control.
\item \textbf{C4 (Honest negatives \& edge cost).} Two rigorously established negatives---spatial coupling does not aid plain forecasting, and an advection--diffusion physics prior gives no significant gain---plus a measured edge-cost profile and CPU-only reproducible code.
\end{itemize}

The remainder: Section~\ref{sec:related} reviews related work; Section~\ref{sec:data} the testbed, sensing graph, and tasks; Section~\ref{sec:method} the model and the three levers; Section~\ref{sec:setup} the protocol; Section~\ref{sec:results} results; Sections~\ref{sec:discussion}--\ref{sec:conclusion} discuss and conclude.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
% =========================================================================
\textbf{Edge intelligence and the sensor budget.} Learning on and near resource-constrained IoT devices is now a broad field \cite{murshed2021edge}; the dominant concern is doing more inference with less hardware. Our contribution is orthogonal to model compression: we reduce the \emph{physical-sensor} budget by software inference, transfer, and fault tolerance, using one lightweight model across deployment sizes.

\textbf{Virtual and soft sensing.} Estimating an unmeasured quantity from correlated measured ones is the classical soft-sensor problem, increasingly addressed with deep learning \cite{sun2021softsensor}. Spatially, inferring a field at unsensored locations is spatiotemporal kriging; inductive graph approaches such as KCN \cite{appleby2020kcn} and IGNNK \cite{wu2021ignnk} learn to interpolate over a sensor graph and generalize to unseen nodes. We adopt this inductive view but situate it inside a sensor-budget framing and pair it with cross-deployment transfer and degradation analysis, and we test---and reject---an explicit transport-physics prior for the regime of sparse, far-apart nodes.

\textbf{Graph learning for environmental sensing.} PM2.5-GNN \cite{wang2020pm25gnn} introduced wind-aware edges for pollutant forecasting; AirFormer \cite{liang2023airformer} added stochastic transformers. These target single, fixed, dense networks and the forecasting task. Low-cost distributed air-quality sensing \cite{morawska2018lowcost} is precisely the sparse-deployment regime our methods address.

\textbf{Inductive GNNs and cross-domain transfer.} GraphSAGE \cite{hamilton2017graphsage} and GAT \cite{velickovic2018gat} established inductive message passing; GIN \cite{xu2019gin} grounds size-invariant readouts. Domain-adversarial training \cite{ganin2016dann} aligns feature distributions across domains. We use the inductive property to transfer one operator across deployments of very different size, and benchmark it honestly against a station-independent recurrent transferer.

\textbf{Sensing graph.} A deployment induces a directed graph $\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E},W)$ whose $N$ nodes are sensor nodes. An edge $(i\!\rightarrow\!j)$ exists iff node $i$ is among the $k{=}3$ nearest neighbours of $j$ by haversine distance $d_{ij}$, weighted by Gaussian decay
\begin{equation}
w_{ij}=\exp\!\left(-\,d_{ij}^{2}/(2\sigma^{2})\right),\qquad \sigma=5~\text{km},
\label{eq:edge}
\end{equation}
giving constant average degree 3 ($|\mathcal{E}|=kN$). Only the weights are deployment-specific; the operator that consumes them is shared. A wind-aware variant orients edges along prevailing transport.

\textbf{Climatology-residual signal.} Let $c_n(m,h)$ be the per-(month, hour) mean signal at node $n$ on the training slice. The network regresses the standardized anomaly
\begin{equation}
\tilde{y}_{n,t}=\big(\,(y_{n,t}-c_n(m_t,h_t))-\mu_{\mathrm{tr}}\big)/\sigma_{\mathrm{tr}},
\label{eq:resid}
\end{equation}
inverted at evaluation, which removes the dominant diurnal/seasonal mean shift and renders the residual approximately stationary.

\textbf{Two inference tasks.} With $N$ nodes, $F$ features, and history $H{=}8$ steps (24\,h), we study (i) \emph{forecasting}, mapping the recent window to the next-step signal at every node, and (ii) \emph{virtual sensing}, inferring the signal at \emph{unsensored} nodes:
\begin{equation}
f_{\theta}:\ \mathbb{R}^{H\times N\times F}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N},
\qquad
g_{\theta}:\ (X_{\mathcal{S}})\mapsto \hat{y}_{\mathcal{U}},
\label{eq:tasks}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{S}$ is the sensored set and $\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{V}\setminus\mathcal{S}$ is unsensored. The architecture instantiates $f_\theta$ so that $|\theta|$ is \emph{independent of} $N$; the same $\theta$ runs on all deployments.

\section{Method: One Inductive Model, Three Levers}\label{sec:method}
% =========================================================================

\subsection{Inductive, Deployment-Size-Agnostic Encoder}\label{sec:encoder}
The encoder is a four-block stack \mbox{TCN\,$\rightarrow$\,GAT\,$\times$2\,$\rightarrow$\,TCN\,$\rightarrow$\,Linear} (Fig.~\ref{fig:encoder}), in the STGCN/Graph-WaveNet family \cite{yu2018stgcn,wu2019graphwavenet}, kept small for CPU compute. A \emph{temporal} 1-D causal convolution summarizes each node's recent hours,
\begin{equation}
\big(\mathrm{TConv}\,x\big)_{n,t,:}=\textstyle\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\Theta_k\,x_{n,\,t-\delta k,\,:}+b,
\label{eq:tconv}
\end{equation}
with dilation $\delta\in\{1,2\}$ \cite{bai2018tcn}; an edge-weighted GAT \cite{velickovic2018gat} refines each node from its neighbours,
\begin{equation}
e_{ij}=\mathrm{LeakyReLU}\!\left(a_s^{\top}Wx_i + a_d^{\top}Wx_j\right)+\log w_{ij},
\label{eq:gat}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{ij}=\frac{\exp(e_{ij})}{\sum_{i'\in\mathcal{N}(j)}\exp(e_{i'j})},\qquad
h_j=\textstyle\sum_{i\in\mathcal{N}(j)}\alpha_{ij}\,Wx_i.
\label{eq:agg}
\end{equation}
Writing $X\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times H\times N\times F}$, the forward pass composes
\begin{equation}
\hat{\tilde{y}} = \big[\mathrm{LayerNorm}\big(\mathrm{TConv}_2(\mathrm{GAT}_2\!\circ\!\mathrm{GAT}_1\!\circ\!\mathrm{TConv}_1(X))\big)\big]_{:,H}\,W_o,
\label{eq:forward}
\end{equation}
the GAT layers acting on each of the $B{\cdot}H$ per-step graphs. No parameter's shape contains $N$: the temporal kernels act per node, the GAT projections per node/edge, and the head along features. Hence one checkpoint loads onto $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{Delhi}}$ (40), $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{Kolkata}}$ (10), and $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{Guwahati}}$ (4) unchanged---the mechanical precondition for transfer. Table~\ref{tab:edge} reports the measured edge-deployment cost.

\subsection{Lever 1 --- Virtual Sensing}\label{sec:vsense}
Meteorology is available at all nodes (e.g., from reanalysis); only the target is sensor-specific. We split $\mathcal{V}$ into $k$ sensored nodes $\mathcal{S}$ and $\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{V}\setminus\mathcal{S}$ unsensored, mask the target input channel at $\mathcal{U}$, and train by masked reconstruction: at each step a random subset $\mathcal{R}\subset\mathcal{S}$ is \emph{also} masked and the model is supervised to reconstruct it, teaching spatial inference from masked context. The objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}\big(\hat{y}_i-y_i\big)^2 \;+\; \lambda\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{phys}},
\label{eq:vsense}
\end{equation}
with the field reconstructed over the whole graph and evaluated only at held-out $\mathcal{U}$. The optional physics term $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{phys}}$ (Section~\ref{sec:physics}) is a steady-state transport residual; the data-only case is $\lambda{=}0$.

\subsection{Lever 2 --- Cross-Deployment Cold-Start Transfer}\label{sec:transfer}
A freshly deployed network has too little labelled history to train a deep model from scratch. Cold-start transfer removes that wait by \emph{inheriting} a model from an existing dense deployment, in three stages summarized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:transfer}.

\textbf{What transfers, and why it is possible.} Every learnable tensor of the encoder $\theta$ has a shape fixed by $(F,d_h,d_g,K)$ with \emph{no} dependence on the node count $N$ (Section~\ref{sec:encoder}). The same $\theta$ therefore instantiates, without any reshaping, on a target graph of any size---a checkpoint trained on Delhi's 40-node graph loads \emph{verbatim} onto Guwahati's 4-node graph. What is carried across is not node-specific state but two reusable \emph{operators}: the temporal-convolution kernels of \eqref{eq:tconv}, which encode how a node's recent hours evolve, and the GAT projections of \eqref{eq:gat}--\eqref{eq:agg}, which encode how a node should aggregate evidence from its neighbours. Because pollutant dynamics and spatial coupling are broadly similar across deployments, a target network can \emph{adopt} these operators rather than relearn them, and only a light target gradient is needed to specialize them.

\textbf{Stage 0 (pre-train).} The encoder is trained to convergence on the full source deployment---MSE in climatology-residual space, Adam, weight decay $10^{-5}$, gradient clipping at norm 1, and best-by-validation checkpointing---producing source weights $\theta_s$.

\textbf{Stage 1 (transfer/fine-tune).} $\theta_s$ is loaded into a target-instantiated encoder and fine-tuned on a fraction $d\in\{15,30,45,60\}\%$ of the target's labelled windows. So that the small, noisy target gradient does not wash out the transferred low-level filters, the \emph{input} temporal block is frozen for the first 20\% of fine-tune epochs \cite{yosinski2014} and then released, letting adaptation flow from the spatial layers inward. Fine-tuning costs minutes on a CPU---the operational meaning of a ``cold start.''

\textbf{What the LSTM baseline can and cannot carry.} The station-independent LSTM transferer follows the identical Stage-0/Stage-1 recipe (without the freeze), so the comparison isolates the effect of the graph. The decisive difference is \emph{what} each model transfers: the LSTM carries only a shared per-node univariate forecaster, whereas the ST-GNN additionally carries the \emph{between-node} aggregation operator---the component that powers virtual sensing (Lever~1) and that a station-independent model structurally cannot possess.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{Cold-start transfer with three-way verification, per $(\text{source},\text{target},d)$ cell}
\label{alg:transfer}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Input:} source checkpoint $\theta_s$; target deployment $t$; target fraction $d$
\STATE $(X,y)\leftarrow$ target labelled windows;\ \ $\mathrm{keep}\leftarrow$ seeded $d$-subsample of indices
\STATE $R^2_{zs}\leftarrow \mathrm{eval}(\theta_s,\,t.\mathrm{test})$ \COMMENT{zero-shot: source weights, no fine-tune}
\STATE $\theta_{tl}\leftarrow \mathrm{finetune}(\theta_s,\,X_{\mathrm{keep}},y_{\mathrm{keep}})$;\ \ $R^2_{tl}\leftarrow \mathrm{eval}(\theta_{tl},\,t.\mathrm{test})$
\STATE $\theta_{sc}\leftarrow \mathrm{trainScratch}(X_{\mathrm{keep}},y_{\mathrm{keep}})$;\ \ $R^2_{sc}\leftarrow \mathrm{eval}(\theta_{sc},\,t.\mathrm{test})$
\STATE \textbf{return} $\big(R^2_{tl}>R^2_{zs}\big)\wedge\big(R^2_{tl}>R^2_{sc}\big)$ \COMMENT{real transfer?}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Three-Way Verification Protocol}\label{sec:verify}
Reporting only $R^2_{tl}$ cannot separate ``the source helped'' from ``we merely trained on $d\%$ of the target.'' We therefore require every transfer cell to beat \emph{both} controls in Algorithm~\ref{alg:transfer}---the zero-shot baseline ($R^2_{zs}$: source weights, no fine-tune) and the from-scratch baseline ($R^2_{sc}$: random init fine-tuned on the \emph{same} sub-sample)---and summarize the cell by its gain over the stronger control,
\begin{equation}
\Delta = R^2_{tl}-\max\!\big(R^2_{zs},\,R^2_{sc}\big)>0.
\label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}
The scratch control is what makes the claim falsifiable: it isolates the benefit of pre-training from the benefit of simply having $d\%$ of the target labels.

\subsection{An Explicit Physics Prior (Tested Honestly)}\label{sec:physics}
On the predicted field $\hat{C}$ we place a steady-state advection--diffusion residual on the sensor graph,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{phys}}=\frac{1}{s^2}\,\big\lVert\,\alpha\,(\mathcal{A}\hat{C})+K\,(\mathcal{L}\hat{C})\,\big\rVert^2,
\label{eq:phys}
\end{equation}
with learnable $\alpha,K\!\ge\!0$, an upwind advection operator on measured wind, $(\mathcal{A}C)_i=\sum_j u_{ij}(C_i-C_j)$, $u_{ij}=[v_i\cos(\phi^{\mathrm{flow}}_i-\theta_{ij})]_+/d_{ij}$, and a graph-Laplacian diffusion $(\mathcal{L}C)_i=\sum_j W_{ij}(C_i-C_j)$. Acting purely spatially, it should propagate concentration from sensored anchors to $\mathcal{U}$ along transport paths. Section~\ref{sec:results} shows it does \emph{not} help significantly in this regime.

\subsection{Computational Complexity and Edge Deployability}\label{sec:complexity}
\textbf{Cost.} A forward pass costs
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{O}\!\big(B H\,(N d_h^2 + |\mathcal{E}|\,d_g)\big),
\label{eq:complexity}
\end{equation}
the temporal convolutions \eqref{eq:tconv} contributing $\mathcal{O}(N d_h^2)$ per step and the sparse attention \eqref{eq:gat}--\eqref{eq:agg} contributing $\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{E}|\,d_g)$---\emph{linear in the number of edges}. Because the $k$-NN graph has $|\mathcal{E}|=kN$, the encoder scales \emph{linearly} in the node count: densifying a deployment raises cost proportionally, not with the quadratic blow-up of a dense pairwise model. The parameter budget is $\mathcal{O}(F d_h K + d_h d_g)$ with no $N$ term, so the footprint is constant across deployment sizes (Table~\ref{tab:edge}).

\textbf{Edge versus cloud.} These constants are small enough to relocate inference from the cloud to the deployment itself. The measured profile---23.7k parameters, 92.8\,KB, and ${<}2.6$\,ms per full-network inference on one CPU core (Table~\ref{tab:edge}), with no GPU, accelerator, or graph library---fits a gateway-class device. Running the model in-network changes what must cross the uplink: instead of streaming every node's multivariate record to a data centre ($N{\times}F$ values per step), a gateway emits only the inference product---per-node forecasts, virtual-sensing estimates, or threshold-crossing alerts---reducing uplink volume by roughly the feature factor $F$ and removing a cloud round-trip from the alert path. Virtual sensing compounds the saving in hardware: locations served by software sensors need no deployed node at all. Thus accuracy, latency, bandwidth, and capital cost are improved together by one $|V|$-independent model---the property that makes the approach deployable at IoT scale rather than only in a data centre.

\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:setup}
% =========================================================================
\textbf{Splits.} A deterministic interleaved 70/15/15 split (every 7th step to validation/test, disjoint targets) is used because the one-year deployments would otherwise yield a winter-only chronological test partition; method rankings, which share the protocol, are unaffected. The validation$-$test \Rtwo gap averages $-0.005$ (test slightly higher), indicating no overfitting.

\textbf{Protocol \& software.} Scalers and per-(month, hour) climatology are fit on the training mask only; an in-loader imputation was patched to be strictly causal. All metrics are computed in raw \pmf space after inverse standardization and climatology add-back. The implementation is PyTorch with NumPy and scikit-learn only---no PyTorch Geometric or compiled extensions---so every grid completes in ${\approx}2.5$\,h on 8 vCPU without a GPU. A complete leakage/overfitting audit is released as supplementary material.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}
% =========================================================================
All metrics are on held-out target nodes/partitions in raw \pmf space.

\subsection{Lever 1: Virtual Sensing Is the Graph Model's Decisive Win}
Table~\ref{tab:vsense} and Fig.~\ref{fig:vsense} report inference at unsensored nodes. The ST-GNN, which borrows from sensored neighbours over the graph, reaches \Rtwo of 0.73--0.77 and beats a meteorology-only station-independent LSTM---which has no spatial channel---by \textbf{+0.158, +0.130, and +0.083} \Rtwo at Delhi (16/40 and 8/40 sensored) and Kolkata (4/10). This is the sensor-budget payoff: four physical sensors in Kolkata software-cover the remaining six locations at \Rtwo${=}0.73$, and sixteen cover forty in Delhi. The station-independent model structurally cannot do this; the graph is non-removable here.

\subsection{Lever 2: Cold-Start Transfer Works---For Both Model Families}
Fig.~\ref{fig:dataeff} verifies transfer across all 24 cells (6 ordered deployment pairs $\times$ 4 fractions): the transfer model beats both zero-shot and from-scratch controls in \textbf{24/24} cells, with the margin largest where target data is scarcest---a new sparse deployment recovers most accuracy from ${\le}15\%$ of its own labels. Table~\ref{tab:cross} compares the two model families at $d{=}30\%$. The station-independent \textbf{LSTM transferer is consistently the stronger forecaster} (by ${\approx}0.01$--$0.04$ \Rtwo), confirming that cold-start forecasting does \emph{not} require the spatial model. Cross-deployment transfer is thus a property of the pre-train/fine-tune recipe, available cheaply; the graph's value lies elsewhere (Levers 1 and 3).

\subsection{Negative 1: Spatial Coupling Does Not Aid Plain Forecasting}
Table~\ref{tab:source} shows source-only single-deployment forecasting: the station-independent LSTM marginally \emph{beats} or ties the GAT-GNN on all three deployments, and swapping the GNN's temporal encoder (TCN$\rightarrow$GRU) does not help (Delhi 0.830). For short-horizon forecasting at sparse, far-apart sensors, between-node coupling adds no value---so a heavy spatial model is not justified by forecasting alone. We treat this as a feature for the sensor-budget thesis: deploy the cheap model for forecasting, and reserve the graph for the tasks (Levers 1, 3) that require it.

\subsection{Negative 2: A Transport-Physics Prior Gives No Significant Gain}
Adding the advection--diffusion residual \eqref{eq:phys} to virtual sensing yields no significant improvement (Fig.~\ref{fig:pinn}). On Kolkata ($k{=}4$, 5 seeds) the mean gain is $\Delta\Rtwo=+0.0034\pm0.0048$ ($p\approx0.19$); advection-only and diffusion-only are statistically indistinguishable, and on Delhi advection-only can hurt. Any marginal effect is generic graph smoothing, not transport physics---an instructive negative for the sparse, irregular regime where collocation-grid PINNs are known to struggle.

% =========================================================================
\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
% =========================================================================
\textbf{A sensor-budget operating procedure.} The results compose into a deployment recipe. To instrument a new region under a tight budget: place $k$ physical nodes, cover the remaining locations by virtual sensing (Lever 1), bring the network online immediately by cold-starting from a dense deployment rather than waiting for local history (Lever 2), and rely on graceful degradation for fault tolerance (Lever 3)---all from one 93\,KB model at ${<}3$\,ms/inference on commodity CPUs, deployable at an edge gateway without GPU or specialized libraries.

\textbf{Where the graph does and does not pay.} Our honest negatives sharpen the recommendation: do not pay for a spatial model to forecast (a station-independent model is as good and cheaper), and do not expect a hand-specified transport prior to help at sparse nodes. The graph's return is concentrated in spatial inference and sparsity robustness, exactly the levers that reduce physical-sensor count.

% =========================================================================
\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
% =========================================================================
Three deployments is a small sample; generalization to other sensing modalities and geographies remains to be tested. The single-year deployments necessitate the interleaved split, which inflates absolute \Rtwo versus chronological-block evaluation (method rankings, sharing the protocol, are unaffected); a chronological sensitivity study is the priority follow-up. Per-cell metrics use a single seed except where multi-seed ablation is reported; broader seed sweeps would tighten the comparisons. Meteorology is assumed available network-wide (realistic via reanalysis); fully sensor-free regions are out of scope.

% =========================================================================
\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% =========================================================================
We reframed spatiotemporal deep learning for distributed sensing as a \emph{sensor-budget} problem and showed, on heterogeneous IoT air-quality deployments of 40, 10, and 4 nodes, that a single inductive, \V-independent, edge-deployable model stretches a small sensor budget three ways: virtual sensing that infers the field at unsensored nodes (+0.08--0.16 \Rtwo over a station-independent baseline), cold-start transfer that brings a new sparse network online from ${\le}15\%$ of its labels (verified 24/24), and graceful degradation that holds above 0.80 \Rtwo down to four sensors. We were candid about where the graph does not help---plain forecasting and a transport-physics prior---so that the positive results carry weight. The framing and the methods are signal-agnostic and apply broadly to sparse, heterogeneous IoT sensing.
